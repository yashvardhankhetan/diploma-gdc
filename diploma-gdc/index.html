Diploma GDC — Support

A graphing calculator and study companion for IB Diploma Mathematics

Need help, found a bug, or want something added? Email diplomagdc@gmail.com. Please say which iPhone or iPad you are using, which version of iOS, and — if something gave a wrong answer — the exact expression you typed. That last detail is usually enough to reproduce and fix it the same day.

Before you email: the question everyone asks first

Can I use this in my IB exam?

No. No phone or tablet is permitted in an IB examination. This app is built for homework, revision and checking your work, and it is deliberately designed so that the habits you build in it transfer to the graphing calculator you are allowed to take in. The Toolkit tab lists the keystroke sequences for the approved handheld calculators for sixteen common operations, so you can practise the same operation on the hardware. Tools that no approved calculator can perform are badged Study only, and the computer algebra system is switched off by default for the same reason.

Common questions

Why is my answer only showing three significant figures?

Three significant figures is the IB reporting convention, so it is the default. Settings will take anything from 1 to 12. Note that intermediate values are never rounded — rounding happens only when a result is displayed — and exact integers are shown in full, so 2^10 is 1024 rather than 1020.

Why did 1/2x give a different answer than I expected?

The app reads implicit multiplication as binding more tightly than an explicit division sign, so 1/2x means 1/(2x). This matches the Casio convention and the way the expression appears in the formula booklet. Some other calculators read it as (1/2)x — the two approved handhelds genuinely disagree with each other here. When it matters, bracket it.

Some tools are missing from the menus.

The course filter in Settings — AA SL, AA HL, AI SL, AI HL — hides everything outside the syllabus you picked, and filters the formula reference to match. Choose Show all to see everything.

How do I turn the computer algebra system on?

Settings, then CAS. It is off by default behind an explanation screen, and while it is on an orange banner sits above every tab. That banner is not a bug and cannot be dismissed: approved exam calculators deliberately exclude a CAS, and it should be obvious when you are relying on one.

Is my work saved? Is it sent anywhere?

It is saved automatically on your device, and it is sent nowhere. The app makes no network requests at all and collects nothing — see the privacy policy. It works fully in Airplane Mode.

How do I type a fraction, a root, or an integral?

Use the structure keys on the keypad. The editor is two-dimensional: fractions stack, roots take a vinculum, and integrals carry their limits. Pressing a structure key captures what you just typed — type 2 then the power key and 2 becomes the base. Backspace walks the structure rather than deleting characters blindly, so from the start of a denominator it moves to the end of the numerator. On a hardware keyboard, typing a slash builds a real fraction.

An unclosed bracket still evaluated.

That is intended. sin(30 evaluates as sin(30), which is what a handheld calculator does when you press EXE. A surplus closing bracket is still reported as an error, because that one is a genuine slip.

Things the app deliberately does not do

  • 3D surfaces of the form z = f(x, y) are not plotted. 3D primitives — lines, planes, spheres, cylinders, cones — are.
  • General implicit relations typed directly, such as x² + y² = 25, are handled through the Conic sections tool rather than a general implicit plotter.
  • Symbolic integration refuses rather than guesses. If it cannot find an antiderivative it says so. For checking your own work, a refusal is more useful than a confident wrong answer.
  • Multi-step undo is not available. History is kept, and any earlier result can be tapped to reuse.
  • Programs cannot be imported. The built-in programming language runs only what you type into the app.

Reporting a wrong answer

Mathematical correctness is the whole point of this app, so a wrong result is the most serious kind of bug it can have and is treated that way. If you find one, send the exact expression, what the app returned, and what you expected. If a markscheme or your teacher disagrees with the app, say so — a few conventions in the app are deliberate judgement calls between two defensible readings, and knowing which one your school teaches is genuinely useful.
